Thermal Interface Heat Transfer Coefficient Testing

Thermal Interface Heat Transfer Coefficient Testing evaluates the ability of an interface between contacting materials or surfaces to transfer thermal energy under defined conditions.

The test can determine the effective heat transfer coefficient, heat flux, temperature difference, interface temperature, thermal resistance and thermal conductance.

Key Parameters

Heat Transfer Coefficient

Effective coefficient describing heat transfer through the defined interface.

Heat Flux

Thermal energy transferred through a unit area of the interface.

Interface Temperature

Temperature measured at or near the thermal contact interface.

Temperature Difference

Difference between defined hot-side and cold-side temperatures.

Thermal Resistance

Resistance to heat transfer across the tested interface.

Thermal Conductance

Total heat-transfer capability of the defined interface.

Contact Pressure

Pressure between the contacting materials during testing.

Interface Area

Effective contact area used for thermal calculations.

Surface Roughness

Surface condition influencing real contact and heat transfer.

Material Pair

Combination of materials forming the tested thermal interface.

Testing Methods

Steady-State Method

Heat transfer coefficient is calculated after stable thermal conditions are reached.

Heat Flow Meter

Measures heat flow together with the thermal gradient across the test assembly.

Guarded Hot Plate

Controlled thermal-gradient method for evaluating heat transfer performance.

Comparative Method

Interface performance is evaluated against a known reference configuration.

Pressure-Controlled Method

Measures thermal behavior at defined interface contact pressures.

Transient Method

Determines thermal response during changing temperature conditions.

Thermal Test Vehicle

Integrated test configuration for practical thermal-interface evaluation.

Real heat-transfer coefficient testing requires calibrated temperature and heat-flow measurement equipment, suitable specimen preparation, controlled contact pressure, defined interface geometry and validated test procedures. Calculated coefficients depend on temperature difference, heat flux, effective area, interface condition, material properties, surface roughness and measurement uncertainty. Laboratory results should not automatically be treated as the performance of a complete installed industrial system.
