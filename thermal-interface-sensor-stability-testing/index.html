Thermal Interface Sensor Stability Testing

Thermal Interface Sensor Stability Testing evaluates whether a sensor maintains a stable measurement over time while the defined thermal and environmental conditions remain controlled.

The test focuses on long-term measurement stability, drift, maximum deviation, trend and measurement change over the observation period.

Unlike repeatability testing, which compares repeated readings, stability testing evaluates how the measurement behaves continuously or periodically over time.

What Is Tested?

Time Stability

Measurement behavior during a defined observation period.

Measurement Drift

Change in sensor output relative to the initial measurement.

Maximum Deviation

Largest observed deviation from the defined reference value.

Trend

Direction and rate of measurement change during the test.

Long-Term Behavior

Sensor response over extended operating periods.

Stability Limit

Comparison of observed variation with the acceptance criterion.

Stability Testing Methods

Continuous Monitoring

Sensor output is recorded continuously during the stability period.

Periodic Monitoring

Measurements are collected at defined time intervals.

Long-Duration Test

Sensor output is observed over an extended period.

Drift Analysis

Measurement change is evaluated against time.

Reference Stability

Sensor readings are compared with a controlled reference.

Trend Analysis

Measurement direction and stability behavior are evaluated.

Quality Decision

PASS

Observed drift and maximum deviation remain within the defined stability limit.

REVIEW

The sensor is approaching the stability limit and requires technical evaluation.

FAIL

Observed drift exceeds the defined stability acceptance limit.
